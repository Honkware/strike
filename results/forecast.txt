  WHY IT DOES NOT CLOSE - is the peak day identifiable at all?

  precision the task demands
    median load gap, a month's #1 day vs its #2        217 MW   (0.26% of peak)
  precision a day-ahead forecast delivers
    walk-forward temperature model                   2,684 MW
    same features fit WITH hindsight (the floor)     2,364 MW
    persistence, 'tomorrow equals today'             2,380 MW

  The error bar is 11x the gap it would have to resolve, and that
  holds even fitting on the answers. The temperature model does not
  beat persistence (2,684 vs 2,380 MW), so these features carry
  almost no marginal information about WHICH hot day sets the record.

  Ranking the top days of a month needs a day-ahead peak forecast
  accurate to 0.26%. The best these features reach, fit on
  the answers, is 2.9% - an order of magnitude coarser. This is
  not a tuning problem. (Published ERCOT day-ahead load MAPE is
  around 2%, consistent with that, but is cited here, not measured.)

  MEASURED CONSEQUENCE: best honest full coverage is 126.5 h/yr
  against a 31.6 h/yr break-even, so curtailment-based 4CP
  avoidance pays only if 75% of the load is deferrable rather
  than lost. Since you cannot know WHICH day, you must be cheap to
  curtail on MANY days - which is precisely what densify.py measured
  at $0 (94% of the saving comes from batching, not from timing).

  The prediction route is closed. The flexibility route is not.
